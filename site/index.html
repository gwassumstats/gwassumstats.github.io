      What is new in the files?

      1. An added study has its GWAS Catalog accession (GCST…) as ID, and New 2026 as release: both are on its page.
      2. M counts the SNPs of its file. Its rg was estimated on a copy without strand-ambiguous SNPs.
      3. N is the sample size of the study; the N of a SNP in the file can differ.
      4. 26 studies are unsigned, 3 of them added in 2026: they have h² but no rg.

      How were h² and rg updated?

      1. h² of the added studies was estimated as in 2024. Pagadala_2025_HypogonadismMale uses a corrected estimate (h² 0.0945, SE 0.009).
      2. rg of the pairs of 2024 is unchanged. Every other pair of signed studies of the same ancestry was estimated in the same way. No pair has one study of each ancestry.

      Which pairs have no rg?

      LDSC gave no estimate for 821 of the 134,940 EUR pairs and 55 of the 1,540 EAS pairs; the site shows them as not estimated. A few studies account for most: Harlow_2026_AnalgesicCessationQ4VsQ1to3Male lacks an estimate with 518 of the 519 other EUR studies, and Purdue_2024_KidneyCancerEAS with 55 of the 55 other EAS studies.

      How were the nonredundant panels chosen?

      1. The study with the most pairs without an estimate is removed, again and again, until every pair left has one: 22 EUR studies and 1 EAS study go, 498 and 55 stay.
      2. Of those, the studies with h² z > 6 are taken from the highest z down. One enters the panel if its squared rg with every study already in it is at most 0.1.

      The EUR panel keeps 66 of its 74 studies of 2024, and 16 of its studies were added in 2026. The EAS panel keeps 11 of 12, with 6 added in 2026. Step 1 is new: the rule of 2024 dropped any study with a pair without an estimate, which now is every study.

      What is not in it?

      GWAS whose sumstats are outside the GWAS Catalog, and papers with more than 100 accessions, are not added yet.

      About GWAS Sumstats

      A catalog of the Price lab's collection of GWAS summary statistics: 602 studies in the format of LDSC, with their heritability (h²) and their genetic correlations (rg). The files are on the O2 cluster; lab members log in to see where. The code is in the public repository.

      How is a study named?

      By a short name, FirstAuthor_Year_Trait: the first author and the year of its paper, and its trait, abbreviated, as in Speliotes_2010_BMI. EAS marks an East Asian study; Female or Male, a study of women or of men only, of a trait that both have. The name is only a label here: a study's page gives its ID in the release, which names its file, and its name in the release. The search finds all three.

      What is in a file?

      One row per SNP, tab-separated, in the .sumstats format of LDSC. Only HapMap3 SNPs.

      ColumnContent
      SNPrsID
      A1, A2The two alleles; the sign of Z is for A1
      NSample size
      ZZ-score of the association

      In the 26 unsigned studies, Z has no direction; 3 of them have no A1 and A2.

      Where is a study's file?

      On O2: a study's page shows the path after login. The file serves for h² and, if the study is signed, for rg. For the studies added in 2026, rg was estimated on a copy without strand-ambiguous SNPs (A/T and C/G), also on O2.

      How were h² and rg estimated?

      1. h²: stratified LD score regression (S-LDSC) with the baseline-LD model v2.2, on the observed scale. Its z is h² over its standard error. The intercept is that of the regression.
      2. rg: cross-trait LD score regression, for every pair of signed studies of the same ancestry.
      3. Both use LD scores of 1000 Genomes Phase 3 for the study's ancestry, EUR or EAS.

      How is a study checked?

      Every study goes through the same checks, and its page says what they found. Of the 602 studies, 529 pass, 54 warn and 19 fail.

      1. Its file, read SNP by SNP: the columns, SNPs listed twice, Z that are not numbers, and how many of the HapMap3 SNPs that LDSC regresses on it has. Fewer than 200,000 fails; fewer than half warns.
      2. Its statistics: a mean χ² below 1.02, an h² within 2 standard errors of 0 or above 1, and an LDSC intercept of 1.1 or more that explains most of the inflation, each warn. An h² of 0 or less fails.
      3. Its label, against its genetic correlations: a study warns if its rg is 0.95 or more with a study of another trait, or stays below 0.6 with a study of the same trait. It fails if other studies confirm the other label and none its own.
      4. Its row, against the GWAS Catalog's record of the study: N, ancestry and paper.
      5. By hand: what someone who looked at a flagged study found, with the evidence.

      A fail means: do not use the study as it is. A warn: use it with care. A note is worth knowing, and the study passes. The checks and their thresholds are in the repository.

      What is the nonredundant panel?

      85 EUR and 17 EAS studies with h² z > 6 and a squared rg of at most 0.1 between any two: traits that are close to independent. The release notes say how they were chosen.

      How do I cite it?

      Cite the paper of each study you use: its reference, which links to PubMed. Tick the studies to download their references as BibTeX. For h² and rg, cite LDSC (Bulik-Sullivan et al. 2015), cross-trait LDSC (Bulik-Sullivan et al. 2015), S-LDSC (Finucane et al. 2015) and the baseline-LD model (Gazal et al. 2017).

      How do I export a list of studies?

      Tick them, in a table or on their pages. Above the table, download their names and IDs as a tab-separated file, with the paths after login, or their references as BibTeX. The ticks stay in this browser.

      How does a study get in?

      A release adds GWAS with public summary statistics. The candidates for the next one are found in the GWAS Catalog by a rule, which is tested on what the last release added; anyone can nominate another there.

      Who made it?

      Gaspard Kerner made the 2026 update. Ben Strober made the 2024 collection, which the update extends.

      Who can see where the files are?

      Lab members, after login with the lab's passphrase. The paths are stored encrypted; the browser decrypts them and keeps the key until logout. Everything else here is public.

      How do I report a problem or give feedback?

      A study's page has a link to report a problem with it: a wrong label, a doubtful file or result. For anything else, open an issue on GitHub. Issues are public: no paths, and nothing from the lab's mailing list.
